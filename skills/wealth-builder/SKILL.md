# Wealth Builder — Post-Debt Wealth Strategy (SA-first)

> **This module is written for South Africa** (the household this system serves). For another country, read `skills/country/[COUNTRY_CODE]/SKILL.md` and substitute its account types — the principles transfer, the wrappers don't.

## Key Premise

Debt freedom is the inflection point. The money that went to creditors now flows to the household. Without a written plan, lifestyle creep takes it. With one, the same discipline that killed the debt builds the wealth.

## Step 3: The SA Wealth Sequence (default order — household's wealth-strategy.md wins if it differs)

### Phase 0 — While expensive debt remains
Don't invest beyond contractual commitments (existing RA debit orders, token TFSA flows) while debt above ~15-20% interest exists. No ETF return reliably beats a 25% loan. Exception: never cancel contractual RAs (penalties), never skip an employer pension match (it's a 50-100% instant return).

### Phase 1 — Safety floor
- Between-pays float and any planned reserves at target.
- **Emergency fund:** start at 1 month of expenses, build to 3, then 6. Stable dual income = 3-4 months is workable; single income or variable = 6+. Keep it in a separate money-market/notice account — boring, liquid, NOT linked to the daily card.

### Phase 2 — Named goals with dates
Goals with a deadline (a move, a vehicle change, school events) get funded BEFORE long-term investing when the date is <24 months out — in cash instruments, not equities. Money needed within 2 years does not belong in the market.

### Phase 3 — TFSA first, every year
- **R46,000/person/tax year (from Mar 2026), R500,000 lifetime.** The TFSA is SA's best wealth wrapper: zero tax on growth, dividends and withdrawals, no Reg 28 constraints.
- Fill it with **low-cost JSE-listed equity index ETFs** (global developed-market tracker as the core, SA top-40 as a satellite), TER ideally <0.4%. NOT cash — a TFSA holding cash wastes the lifetime limit's compounding room.
- Kids' TFSAs: powerful 50+ year compounding, but contributions use THEIR lifetime limit — fund meaningfully or deliberately, not symbolically.

### Phase 4 — RA top-up for the tax deduction
- Deduct up to 27.5% of income (cap R430k from Mar 2026). At a 36-41% marginal rate, every R1,000 contributed costs ~R590-640 after the refund — a strong, immediate return.
- Mind: Reg 28 limits (75% equity / 45% offshore), funds locked to 55, two-pot rules apply. Compare provider fees — a 1%+ total fee RA can forfeit the tax advantage vs a 0.5% one. Ask the adviser to disclose EAC (Effective Annual Cost).
- **Two-pot warning:** the savings pot is emergency-only money taxed at marginal rate on withdrawal. Raiding it for lifestyle is the new payday loan.

### Phase 5 — Discretionary / taxable
EasyEquities or similar taxable account once wrappers are full: same ETFs, now with CGT (R50k annual exclusion from 2026/27, 40% inclusion). Also the layer for property deposits and business capital.

## Step 4: Answer the Actual Question

Anchor to their numbers and timeline. Common ones:
- "When can we start?" → Phase logic above against their debt-freedom date.
- "What ETFs?" → Teach selection criteria (broad index, low TER, global core) — categories, not product punting; final selection is theirs (or with their adviser).
- "Property?" → After safety floor + goal funds; deposit money is Phase 2/5 cash, never the emergency fund.
- "Rand is weak, should we wait?" → DCA principle; you cannot time currency any more than markets.

## Behavioural Checkpoints

Post-debt failure modes: no written plan (cash leaks), perfectionism (researching ETFs for 8 months while money sits in cheque), market fear (cash at 7% feels safe while inflation eats it), windfall splurges (bonuses/refunds skip the plan).
Prevent: write the plan while motivation is high, automate debit orders on payday, start small immediately rather than perfectly later, review yearly not daily.
