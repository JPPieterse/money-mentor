# Investment Principles Reference (SA-first)

Frameworks for post-debt wealth building. Account limits and tax figures live in `skills/country/za/references/tax-and-accounts.md` (verified dates there). Principles below are universal; examples are in rand.

## Emergency Fund Sizing

| Situation | Target | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Stable dual income | 3–4 months | Two incomes, quick recovery |
| Single income, stable job | 6 months | Single failure point |
| Variable/freelance income | 9–12 months | Gaps are structural |
| Major life change coming (move, career switch) | 6–12 months | Transition buffer |

- Count months of *survival* expenses (housing, food, utilities, insurance, school), not full lifestyle.
- Hold it in a separate money-market or 32-day notice account. SA cash rates (~7-8%) make this less painful than in low-rate countries — but cash still loses to equities long-term; the fund is insurance, not investment.
- If tapped, rebuild to target within 3–6 months before resuming investing.

## Account Priority (SA)

1. **Employer pension/provident match** — never leave it; it's a guaranteed 50–100% first-year return.
2. **TFSA (R46k/yr, R500k lifetime)** — zero tax forever, fully flexible, no Reg 28. Fill with equity ETFs, not cash.
3. **RA top-up** — 27.5% deduction (cap R430k): strongest at 36%+ marginal rates. Locked to 55; Reg 28 applies; watch total fees (EAC <1%, ideally ~0.5%).
4. **Taxable account** — overflow, goal funds, property deposits. CGT applies (40% inclusion; R50k annual exclusion).

Dual-earner: each spouse has their own TFSA and RA room — fill both before overflowing to taxable.

## Index Funds / ETFs: Why

- ~85% of active funds underperform their index over 15 years. Fees are the main killer: active 1.0–1.5%+ vs index ETFs 0.1–0.4% TER in SA.
- **R10,000 + R2,000/mo for 30 years at 10% nominal:** ≈ R4.5M. Same at 8.8% (after a 1.2% fee drag): ≈ R3.5M. The "small" fee costs ~R1M.
- One global tracker ETF = thousands of companies, instant diversification, no stock-picking required.

**Simple SA portfolio (categories, not product advice):**
1. Global developed-market equity tracker (MSCI World/S&P 500 class) — the core; also your rand hedge.
2. SA equity tracker (Top 40/Capped SWIX class) — local satellite.
3. Bonds/income (SA bond ETF or money market) — stability layer, grows with age.

**Allocation by horizon:** 25–35yrs old: ~90/10 equity/bonds · 35–50: ~80/20 · 50–65: ~60/40 · retirement: ~40/60. Or by stomach: if a 25% drawdown would make you sell, hold fewer equities than the age rule says.

**Offshore note:** a global ETF bought on the JSE in rand IS offshore exposure (no SDA needed, fine inside a TFSA). Direct offshore investing uses the R2M SDA — a later-stage optimisation, not a starting requirement.

## Rand-Cost Averaging (DCA)

Invest the same rand amount every month, automatically, regardless of market or exchange rate. Low months buy more units, high months fewer; you remove timing and emotion. Lump sums (bonuses, tax refunds): statistically investing immediately beats waiting, but splitting over 3–6 months is fine if it prevents paralysis. Never wait for "the rand to recover" or "the crash" — you cannot time either.

## Rebalancing

Once a year, restore target weights (sell what grew, buy what lagged) — mechanical profit-taking. Inside a TFSA/RA there's no tax cost; in taxable accounts use new contributions to rebalance before selling (avoids CGT events).

## Common SA Mistakes

1. **Timing the market or the rand** — missing the 10 best days in 30 years roughly halves returns.
2. **Cash as a long-term plan** — 7-8% money market feels safe; after ~5-6% inflation and tax it's ~1% real. Equities are volatile but compound.
3. **Single stocks / single sector** — uncompensated risk; the index already owns the winners.
4. **High-fee products** — "guaranteed growth" endowments, smoothed-bonus funds, 3%+ EAC policies. Ask for the EAC in writing; compare to a 0.3% ETF.
5. **Panic selling** — 2008: −40%+; recovered and tripled within a decade. Crashes are the mechanism, not the malfunction.
6. **TFSA misuse** — holding cash in it, or withdrawing (room never comes back; lifetime limit burns).
7. **Two-pot raiding** — savings-pot withdrawals are taxed at marginal rate and amputate compounding; emergencies only.
8. **Status car upgrade at debt freedom** — the classic SA wealth-killer; vehicle finance is how the snowball dies.

## Compound Interest

R2,000/month at 10% nominal: 10 years ≈ R410k · 20 years ≈ R1.5M · 30 years ≈ R4.5M. The third decade earns more than the first two combined — which is why starting NOW with R500 beats starting in five years with R5,000, and why kids' accounts (50-year horizons) are disproportionately powerful.

## Post-Debt Wealth Equation

```
Freed snowball (what used to service debt)
  → 1. Safety: emergency fund to target
  → 2. Dated goals (<24 months): cash instruments
  → 3. TFSA ×2 to annual max (equity ETFs)
  → 4. RA top-up (tax bracket permitting)
  → 5. Taxable investing / property / business
  → 6. Deliberate lifestyle upgrades — chosen, written down, capped
```

A household that frees R20,000/mo and invests even half of it at 10% nominal is a rand millionaire (liquid, ex-property) in ~6 years and holds ~R7.6M after 20. That is why the debt phase was worth it.
